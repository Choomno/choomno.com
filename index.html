The Borromean complement

01 / an experiment in geometry
Background controls

The space between

Remove the Borromean rings from the 3-sphere. What remains admits a complete hyperbolic metric: three cusps, finite volume, and an infinite amount of room.

As in the Geometry Center’s Not Knot, you are looking from inside the complement. Light follows hyperbolic geodesics through the manifold’s face identifications. The slender beams trace the edges of a rhombic dodecahedral fundamental domain. Its six ideal vertices are identified in pairs to give the three cusps: the ring components have receded to infinity.

\[M=S^3\setminus B\cong\mathbb H^3/\Gamma\]

Two ideal octahedra. Three torus cusps.

\(ds^2=\dfrac{4\,(dx^2+dy^2+dz^2)}{(1-x^2-y^2-z^2)^2}\)

This is the complete limit. Optional cusp colors label the ideal ends of the beams; they do not introduce surfaces at a finite distance. The face pairings are exact integer Lorentz matrices for SnapPy’s L6a4. The display uses floating point ray tracing with a finite distance and face-crossing limit.
